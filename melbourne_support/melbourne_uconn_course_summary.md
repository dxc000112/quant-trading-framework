# Suggested UConn Course Evidence for University of Melbourne

This summary organizes the University of Connecticut courses that most directly demonstrate prior study in programming, calculus, linear algebra, probability, stochastic processes, numerical analysis, and mathematical statistics.

Note: This file is a user-friendly summary prepared from official UConn Academic Catalog descriptions. It is not itself an official university-issued syllabus. The official catalog PDFs are available separately:

Recommended primary courses

- CSE 1010: Introduction to Computing for Engineers
  Introductory programming, algorithmic thinking, and computational problem-solving in science, mathematics, and engineering.
- MATH 1131Q: Calculus I
  Limits, continuity, differentiation, and integration, with applications to science and engineering.
- MATH 1132Q: Calculus II
  Transcendental functions, integration techniques, sequences and series, and introductory vector algebra and geometry.
- MATH 2110Q: Multivariable Calculus
  Two- and three-dimensional vector algebra, multivariable calculus, vector differential calculus, and line and surface integrals.
- MATH 2210Q: Applied Linear Algebra
  Systems of equations, matrices, determinants, linear transformations, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors from a computational perspective.
- STAT 3375Q: Introduction to Mathematical Statistics I
  Probability spaces, probability distributions in one and several dimensions, generating functions, and limit theorems.
- MATH 3170 / STAT 3965: Elementary Stochastic Processes
  Conditional distributions, discrete and continuous time Markov chains, random walks, Poisson processes, and Brownian motion.
- MATH 3510: Numerical Analysis I
  Numerical methods for linear systems, eigenvalues, matrix inverses, and roots of nonlinear functions and polynomials.

Optional supporting courses

- MATH 3160: Probability
  Probability axioms, counting, conditional probability, random variables, and limit theorems.
- STAT 3115Q: Analysis of Experiments
  Regression, regression diagnostics, transformations, ANOVA, ANCOVA, and stepwise regression.
- STAT 3445: Introduction to Mathematical Statistics II
  Sampling distributions, parameter estimation, hypothesis testing, correlation, regression, and analysis of variance.
- STAT 3515Q: Design of Experiments
  Experimental design using regression analysis and analysis of variance.
